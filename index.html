05 / REFLECTION

Learning
by doing.

WQE7001 · PERSONAL REFLECTION

Studying cybersecurity has changed the way I think about computer systems. Earlier in my studies, I focused mainly on how networks and applications function. Through WQE7001 and my practical coursework, I have started looking at the same systems from a security perspective: what activity is normal, where weaknesses may appear and how an analyst can gather evidence before reaching a conclusion. I have found that practical exercises make these ideas much easier to understand than reading about them alone.

One important learning experience was configuring a virtual cybersecurity laboratory. Using virtual machines such as Kali Linux and Metasploitable helped me understand why testing environments must be separated from everyday networks. I learned to pay attention to virtual network settings, IP addressing and basic connectivity before attempting any security activity. This reinforced a simple lesson: the quality of a security experiment depends on the reliability of its environment. A misconfigured network can make a test fail even when the tool itself is working correctly.

Another useful topic was network reconnaissance. Working with Nmap introduced me to the relationship between hosts, ports and the services exposed by a system. It also taught me to look beyond the scan output. An open port is an observation, not automatically proof of a vulnerability. Understanding the service, the reason it is exposed and the scope of authorised testing is necessary before drawing conclusions. This has encouraged me to be more careful when documenting findings.

A recurring challenge in practical work has been troubleshooting connectivity and configuration problems. When a virtual lab test did not produce the expected result, the useful approach was to check the network arrangement, review IP settings, test reachability and examine the output again rather than assume the security command was incorrect. Working through problems in that order helped me see the value of verifying assumptions. It also improved my confidence in reading technical output and recording the changes made during testing.

I still find detailed network traffic interpretation challenging, especially when several protocols and events appear together. I understand the basic purpose of packet analysis, but I want to become more consistent at identifying what is significant and explaining why. As a concrete next step, by 28 February 2027 I aim to complete five documented Wireshark practice exercises using permitted sample captures or my own lab traffic. For each exercise, I will record the objective, important packets or protocol observations and a short interpretation. I will measure progress by keeping all five write-ups in my learning notes and checking whether I can explain each finding without relying only on a tutorial.

Overall, this course has made me appreciate cybersecurity as a process of observation, testing, analysis and reflection. I want to continue developing that mindset by practising regularly and presenting my results clearly, including limitations and lessons learned.

My career goal is to become a Cybersecurity Analyst, with a focus on network security, security monitoring and threat investigation. I am interested in the work analysts do to understand unusual events, identify risks and communicate findings that support better security decisions.

My next steps are to deepen my practical experience with traffic analysis and security monitoring tools, continue building documented projects during my MSc studies, and work toward a relevant entry-level cybersecurity certification when I am ready to meet its requirements.
